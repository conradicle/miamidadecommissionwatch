Take action locally

Engage with local government

Five ways to weigh in on what the Board decides, whether or not you ever talk to us.

1. Read the agenda before the meeting

Full BCC and committee agendas are published in advance through the county's legislative system. Our Meeting Tracker highlights select items, but the full agenda always has more.

Browse legislation ↗

2. Speak at public comment

Members of the public can address the Board on agenda items. You can attend the meeting in person, or, when listed on the meeting notice, leave a voice message ahead of time on the phone number provided for that meeting.

3. Watch a meeting

Meetings are webcast when available, so you can follow along without attending in person.

Watch meetings ↗

4. Contact your commissioner's office

Each commissioner's office can take calls, emails, and meeting requests from constituents in their district. Start from the directory.

Commissioner directory

5. Follow along between meetings

We post plain-language summaries after each meeting we cover. Check back on the tracker, or reach out if you'd like to be notified.

Read our updates

This page describes the general public-participation process as published by Miami-Dade County. Specific procedures (sign-up deadlines, comment time limits, phone numbers) can vary by meeting, so always check the notice for the specific meeting you plan to attend or comment on.
Join the team

Join Commission Watch

Reading along is step one. If you want to help build this project directly, here's how.

Become a Watchdog

Watchdog is our recognition for volunteers who go beyond reading along: who write, show up, and represent Commission Watch in person. Do all three, and you'll earn a pair of our branded binoculars.

1. Write an editorial

Submit an opinion piece on a Commission decision or piece of county legislation and get it published on the site.

Submit an editorial

2. Attend a BCC meeting with us

Come to a Board of County Commissioners meeting in person alongside the team. Check the Meeting Tracker for the next one, then reach out to join us there.

3. Wear the binoculars

Do both, and we'll send you a pair of branded Miami-Dade Commission Watch binoculars: the mark of a Watchdog. Wear them to your next meeting.

Done both already? Let us know and we'll get your binoculars to you.

Start a local chapter

The Board of County Commissioners isn’t the only body making decisions where you live — your city commission meets too, and almost nobody covers it. We’re building the structure for student teams to run their own city’s Commission Watch site, on its own address, under the same nonpartisan standards. No city has an active team yet, so the first one is genuinely first.
