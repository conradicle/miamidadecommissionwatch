Regular Meeting · July 21, 2026

Board approves $2.5 billion in borrowing, one no vote

Commissioners authorized six separate bond packages totaling more than $2.5 billion in a single meeting, funding everything from transit to the fight over the Fisher Island fuel depot, and gave final approval to new rules for e-bikes and scooters on county streets.

Passed, 1 dissent

$2.5 billion in new county borrowing, across six bond packages

In one vote, the Board authorized new bonds for transit projects ($350 million), general capital projects ($380 million), voter-approved infrastructure ($350 million), Jackson Health System projects including a new electronic records system ($275 million), parks and public safety facilities ($480 million), and the seaport, including the county's fight to acquire the Fisher Island fuel depot ($700 million). Source: Miami Today.

Why it matters Most of this debt is repaid through dedicated taxes and port revenue rather than your property tax bill directly, but it locks in the county's spending priorities for decades. Commissioner Raquel Regalado cast the only no vote, warning about financing costs at over 6% interest and asking, "I don't understand why we're doing this at this moment... this can wait."
Adopted on second reading

New rules for e-bikes and scooters on county streets

The Board gave final approval to an ordinance regulating micromobility devices, e-bikes, and motorized scooters on county rights-of-way, including how cities can enforce the rules and what civil penalties apply. Source: official meeting notice.

Why it matters If you ride, rent, or just share the road with an e-bike or scooter anywhere in the county, this is the rulebook that now applies.
Regular Meeting · June 16, 2026

Mental health center funded, Fisher Island fuel fight escalates

Commissioners unanimously approved funding for Miami-Dade's long-delayed mental health diversion center, and separately voted to let the Mayor pursue eminent domain to take over the Fisher Island fuel depot that supplies PortMiami.

Passed, unanimous

Miami Center for Mental Health and Recovery gets funded, decades after it was promised

The Board unanimously approved a roughly $31 million, three-year agreement with the nonprofits WestCare Foundation and The Advocate Program to operate a mental health diversion center, with an option to renew for two more years. Commissioners added an outpatient treatment component, a commitment to treat substance abuse alongside mental illness, and independent oversight from Jackson Health System. Source: WLRN.

Why it matters This facility is meant to be an alternative to jail for people in mental health crises, something Miami-Dade has talked about building for over two decades. It's funded partly through Florida's opioid settlement money.
Passed

Board votes to pursue seizing the Fisher Island fuel depot

Commissioners voted to authorize Mayor Daniella Levine Cava to acquire the roughly 10-acre fuel depot on Fisher Island through eminent domain, funded by Seaport Revenue Bonds rather than general tax dollars. The site's owner, HRP Group, said it would fight the move in court. Source: WLRN.

Why it matters PortMiami has no other fuel source for its cruise and cargo ships. Read our full explainer on how this fight got here.
Regular Meeting · June 2, 2026

Boat anchoring rules and Live Local Act housing paperwork

A lighter agenda than the two meetings that followed it: commissioners took up new overnight-anchoring rules for boats in county waterways and local procedures for Florida's Live Local Act affordable housing law.

Item 4B

New limits on overnight boat anchoring

An ordinance up for first reading would set new regulations for overnight anchoring of boats in Miami-Dade's waterways. First reading means it still needs a second, final vote before it takes effect. Source: official meeting agenda.

Why it matters If you live on the water or anchor out overnight, this is the kind of rule that can affect where you're allowed to tie up.
Item 7B

New procedures for Florida's Live Local Act housing covenants

The Board took up, on second reading, an ordinance covering how the county handles covenants tied to Florida's Live Local Act, the state law that lets developers build denser housing if a share of it is priced as affordable. Source: official meeting agenda.

Why it matters The Live Local Act has become one of the biggest levers for adding affordable housing in Florida, and this ordinance sets the local paperwork trail that's supposed to keep developers honest about the deal.
