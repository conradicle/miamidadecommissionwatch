Local Government 101

Your county, explained simply

You don't need a background in government to follow a decision or speak up. Start with who does what, then pick one way to get involved.

Start with your address

Miami-Dade County includes cities, towns, villages, and neighborhoods outside any city's boundaries. The City of Miami is one municipality inside the county. Miami Beach and Coral Gables have their own city governments, too.

The county handles services across the whole region. In unincorporated areas (places outside a city or town), it also provides city-type services. If you live inside a municipality, that city's government makes decisions about local services and rules as well. The right office to contact depends on your address and your issue.

Who makes the decisions?

The Board of County Commissioners

Usually shortened to BCC, this is the county's 13-member commission. Each commissioner represents one district. The Board passes county laws, sets policy, and decides how public money is spent.

The County Mayor

The Mayor runs the county's day-to-day operations and can veto commission actions. A veto is a formal rejection of an action.

County departments and staff

Departments run county programs and provide services. A single proposal can involve both a policy decision by elected officials and practical work by staff.

Follow one decision at a time

A long agenda gets easier once you start with one topic you care about. Here's one way to follow it.

  1. Find an item. Look for something that affects your neighborhood, like a transit project or a land use change. Write down its agenda item number so you can find it again.
  2. Read what is proposed. Our Agenda Summaries explain items before each meeting and link to the county's original documents.
  3. Follow the meeting. Listen for your item, the discussion, and any changes to the proposal. The agenda only tells you what is scheduled, so check the recorded outcome afterward.
  4. Check what happened. Our Meeting Summaries explain outcomes and any vote counts that are available. Look for whether the item passed, was changed, was deferred, or needs another vote.

Try it with a published agenda and the recap that goes with it.

You can take part

Contact your commissioner

Pick one issue. Explain how it affects you, ask a specific question, and say what you'd like the office to do. Use the contact information on the commissioner's official profile.

Find your commissioner →

Attend a meeting

Anyone can attend a BCC meeting to listen and learn. Check the official calendar and agenda for the date, location, and participation rules before you go.

See the county's posted agendas ↗

Give public comment

Public comment is your chance to speak to the Board about eligible agenda items. Speakers have to attend in person and register with staff, and the registration deadline depends on the type of item. Read the county's instructions and arrive early.

Prepare a short statement with the item number, how the issue affects you, and what you want the Board to do. Stick to the speaking time and instructions given at the meeting.

Source: Official speaker registration and public comment instructions ↗

Want to go with other students? You can attend with the Commission Watch team. Signing up with us is separate from the county's speaker registration.

A few words, translated

Agenda
The list of items scheduled for a meeting.
Ordinance
A county law, or a proposal to create or change one.
Resolution
A formal Board action, often used for agreements, spending, or policy decisions.
Public hearing
A scheduled chance for people to comment on a specific matter.
Deferred
Postponed to a later meeting.
First reading
The first stage for a proposed ordinance. Passing first reading is one step toward adoption.
