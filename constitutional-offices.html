Constitutional Offices

The officials elected alongside the Commission

Miami-Dade County also elects seven other officials whose offices are created directly by the Florida Constitution rather than by the county charter. They run independently of the Board of County Commissioners and the Mayor, each with their own budget, staff, and area of responsibility.

Why these offices are separate from the Commission

The Board of County Commissioners is a legislative body created by Miami-Dade's home rule charter. The offices below are different: the Florida Constitution requires every county to elect a Property Appraiser, Tax Collector, Clerk of the Courts and Comptroller, Supervisor of Elections, and Sheriff, and bars county charters from abolishing them or changing how they're chosen. State Attorney and Public Defender are elected separately for the 11th Judicial Circuit, which covers the same area as the county.

Most of these are new to Miami-Dade voters. For decades, the county's home rule charter let the Commission appoint people to run several of these functions instead of holding elections for them. Florida's 2018 constitutional amendment, known as Amendment 10, ended that option for charter counties. Miami-Dade held its first elections for a Property Appraiser, Tax Collector, Supervisor of Elections, and Sheriff in November 2024, and every winner took office on January 7, 2025. The Clerk of Courts had already been an elected post; the role was expanded to include Comptroller duties around the same time.
