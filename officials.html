Executive and constitutional offices

Mayor and constitutional officers

The Mayor runs county departments. The seven constitutional officers are created by the Florida Constitution and run independently of the Mayor and the Commission, each with its own budget and staff. State Attorney and Public Defender serve the 11th Judicial Circuit, which covers the same area as the county.

Why most of these offices are new to Miami-Dade voters

For decades the county charter let the Commission appoint people to run several of these functions instead of holding elections. Florida's 2018 constitutional amendment, known as Amendment 10, ended that option for charter counties. Miami-Dade held its first elections for Tax Collector, Supervisor of Elections and Sheriff in November 2024. The Property Appraiser had been elected on a nonpartisan ballot since 2008 and became a partisan constitutional office in the same election. The amendment took effect in Miami-Dade on January 7, 2025, the day the 2024 winners took office. The Clerk of Courts was already elected; the role gained Comptroller duties around the same time.

Countywide questions

Each needed a majority of the votes cast on it (Charter, Section 9.07(D); Resolution R-440-26 for the School Board question). Tap a question for what it does.

Referendum 1: Independent Salary CommissionPassed51.58% yes

Replaces the fixed Commissioner salary in the charter ($6,000 a year) with a five-member Salary Commission that sets Commissioner pay each year. Members are appointed by the Chief Judge of the 11th Circuit, the State Attorney, the Public Defender, the Ethics Commission's executive director and the Inspector General; the Board can't change its decisions. Yes 370,059, No 347,381. Resolution R-680-26 ↗

Referendum 2: Longer district residency to run for CommissionPassed76.98% yes

Candidates must now live in their district for at least one year before qualifying, up from six months. Yes 570,533, No 170,568. Resolution R-681-26 ↗

Referendum 3: Vacancy elections and city qualifying standardsPassed73.64% yes

When a Mayor or Commissioner resigns to run for another office, the Board can hold the special election alongside another scheduled election. Also sets minimum qualifying-period standards for city candidates. Yes 538,129, No 192,634. Resolution R-682-26 ↗

Referendum 4: Charter updates for the elected constitutional officesPassed68.56% yes

Updates the charter for the elected Sheriff, Tax Collector, Property Appraiser and Supervisor of Elections, clarifies which finance duties belong to the Clerk and which to the County, and requires County employees to take leave when running for a constitutional office. Yes 489,394, No 224,414. Resolution R-683-26 ↗

Referendum 5: Miscellaneous and technical changesPassed63.43% yes

Updates the names of County locations in the charter, allows more ways to give public notice and take public participation where state law allows, and cleans up numbering and grammar. Yes 451,047, No 260,101. Resolution R-684-26 ↗

Referendum 6: More advertising allowed in parksFailed39.59% yes

Would have removed the rule that advertising in charter-protected parks be tied to sponsorship of park events or facilities. Advertising in those parks stays limited to sponsorships. Yes 291,656, No 445,126. Resolution R-685-26 ↗

School Board: renew the 1-mill levy for teacher pay and school safetyPassed81.82% yes

Continues the existing 1-mill property tax ($1 per $1,000 of taxable value) for Miami-Dade County Public Schools, including charter schools, from July 1, 2027 through June 30, 2031. The money goes to teacher and instructional staff pay and school security, with a Citizen Advisory Committee overseeing it. Yes 595,224, No 132,230. Resolution R-440-26 ↗

Florida constitutional amendments

An amendment needs 60% of the votes cast statewide to pass (Florida Constitution, Article XI, Section 5(e)). Statewide totals are from Florida Election Watch and stay unofficial until the state certifies them.

Amendment 1: Budget Stabilization FundFailing40.9% yes statewide

Would have raised the cap on the state's rainy-day fund from 10% to 25% of general revenue and required yearly transfers into it. Miami-Dade: 40.29% yes. Statewide (unofficial): 3,173,724 yes, 4,585,490 no. Division of Elections ↗

Amendment 2: Tax exemption for agricultural equipmentFailing58.21% yes statewide

Would have exempted from property tax the equipment and other tangible personal property used on agricultural land. A majority voted yes, but short of the 60% needed. Miami-Dade: 56.24% yes. Statewide (unofficial): 4,547,114 yes, 3,264,246 no. Division of Elections ↗

Amendment 3: Bigger homestead exemption, lower cap on other propertyPassing61.98% yes statewide

Raises the homestead exemption for non-school property taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, then adjusts it for inflation; cuts the yearly cap on assessment increases for non-homestead property from 10% to 5%; and limits what county and city property taxes can pay for. Takes effect January 1, 2027 once certified. Miami-Dade: 65.57% yes. Statewide (unofficial): 4,922,400 yes, 3,019,415 no. Division of Elections ↗

Earlier in 2026: the August 18 primary

Eight Commission seats were on the 2026 ballot. Five were decided in June when only one candidate qualified: Steve Gallon III (District 1 special election), Micky Steinberg (4), Natalie Milian Orbis (6), Anthony Rodriguez (10) and Juan Carlos "JC" Bermudez (12). Three went to voters on August 18 (official results, certified August 25):

  • District 2: Marleine Bastien re-elected with 78.29% (14,272). Miguel "Skip" Quintero 12.89% (2,349), Ernst Jean Louis 8.83% (1,609).
  • District 5: no majority. Vicki L. Lopez 42.33% (7,064) and Rob Piper 29.54% (4,929) went to the November runoff; Joe Sanchez 28.13% (4,695) was eliminated.
  • District 8: Danielle Cohen Higgins re-elected with 55.07% (10,294). John Edward DuBois 28.15% (5,262), Martha "Vega" Hero 16.78% (3,136).

School Board: Districts 2, 4 and 6 drew one candidate each, and Monica Colucci won District 8 in August.

Voter guide

How to register and vote

  • Register, or check your registration

    U.S. citizens who live in Florida and are 18 by Election Day can register online, by mail or in person. The lookup also shows your precinct and polling place.

    ↗

  • Vote by mail

    Request a ballot by the deadline for that election. Your completed ballot must reach the Supervisor of Elections by 7:00 p.m. on Election Day; a postmark isn't enough.

    ↗

  • Vote early or on Election Day

    During early voting you can use any site in the county. On Election Day you vote at your assigned precinct.

    ↗

  • Find your district

    Know your Commission district before you look up candidates or your sample ballot.
